## Purpose

This specification defines how the Comprehensive Alignment System (CAS) uses the geometric duality of the dodecahedron and icosahedron to achieve recursive, scale‑invariant governance. It establishes the structural rules for nesting the architecture across any level of complexity — from individual to hemispheric — without loss of coherence or centralised control.

The specification is an extension of the published CAS architecture. It does not replace or alter the core primitives, the 10 Immutable Laws, or the CAS v2 License.

---

## 1. Structural Basis: Dodecahedron–Icosahedron Duality

The dodecahedron (12 pentagonal faces, 20 vertices) and the icosahedron (20 triangular faces, 12 vertices) are geometric duals. The vertices of one correspond to the face centres of the other. This duality enables two complementary functions:

- **Dodecahedron (Containment)** – defines the outer boundary of a system. It has 12 faces, so it imposes 12 boundary conditions. It is the stable container.
- **Icosahedron (Mapping)** – defines the internal relational network. It has 20 faces, so it provides 20 relational nodes. It is the dynamic mapper.

The two are inseparable. Containment without mapping is brittle. Mapping without containment is chaotic. Together they form a self‑regulating structure.

---

## 2. Recursive Nesting Principle

Because the dodecahedron and icosahedron are duals, they can be nested recursively. Each level of governance uses the same geometric pair. The vertices of the inner icosahedron align with the face centres of the outer dodecahedron, and vice versa. This creates a fractal architecture in which the same structural logic repeats at every scale.

The nesting is not hierarchical in the sense of authority. Each level governs its own domain. No level can override the level below it. Coherence emerges from the structural relationship between levels, not from a central controller.

## 3. Scale‑Invariant Governance Levels

The architecture is designed to operate at the following scales. Each level has its own containment (dodecahedron) and mapping (icosahedron) layer.

| Level | Containment (Dodecahedron) | Mapping (Icosahedron) | Typical Scale |
|-------|----------------------------|------------------------|---------------|
| Individual | Personal sovereignty boundary | Personal relational mapping | 1 person |
| Family | Family boundary | Family relational mapping | 2–20 |
| Residential | Residential boundary | Community mapping | 20–500 |
| Suburb | Suburb boundary | Suburb relational mapping | 500–50k |
| City | City boundary | City relational mapping | 50k–5M |
| State/Province | State boundary | State relational mapping | 5M–50M |
| Nation | National boundary | National relational mapping | 50M–1B+ |
| Region | Regional boundary | Regional relational mapping | Multi‑nation |
| Hemisphere | Hemispheric boundary | Hemispheric relational mapping | Global |

Each level is complete in itself. It has its own enforcement, its own attribution, and its own escalation paths. When a level needs to coordinate with another, it does so through defined interfaces (see Section 5).

## 4. Nesting Mechanics

Nesting occurs through the alignment of dual vertices. The process is as follows:

1. **Define the outer dodecahedron** – establish the 12 boundary conditions for the level (e.g., legal constraints, ethical limits, resource caps).
2. **Inscribe the icosahedron** – map the 20 relational nodes to the internal actors, processes, or data flows within the boundary.
3. **Align the duals** – ensure that each vertex of the icosahedron corresponds to a face centre of the dodecahedron. This creates a stable correspondence between containment and mapping.
4. **Nest the next level** – the inner icosahedron can itself contain a smaller dodecahedron, which in turn contains an icosahedron, and so on.

The nesting can continue until the Truncated Demicube Horizon is reached. That horizon is the theoretical cap of geometric alignment and is gated until global consensus is established.

## 5. Inter‑Level Interfaces

Although levels are autonomous, they must coordinate. Coordination occurs through two mechanisms:

- **Boundary handshake** – adjacent levels exchange a defined set of boundary conditions. For example, a city level may share resource limits with a state level, but neither can override the other's internal mapping.
- **Attribution bridge** – the Load Attribution Engine (LAE) records contributions across levels. When a decision at one level affects another, the LAE logs the cross‑level load and maintains provenance.

These interfaces are defined by the Rosetta Layer, which translates concepts across domains without loss of meaning.

---

## 6. Enforcement and Verification

Each level enforces its own boundaries through the Boundary Enforcement Layer (BEL). The BEL operates at the kernel level (via System77) for computational systems, or through policy and contract for human systems. Enforcement is deterministic: actions that violate the boundary are blocked before execution.

Verification is performed by the Degradation Signal Monitor (DSM), which watches for rising risk at each level and across levels. If a level breaches a threshold, the Governance Escalation Protocol (GEP) triggers a graduated response, from soft guidance to human takeover.

The recursive architecture ensures that verification scales: each level verifies itself, and the levels together verify the whole.

## 7. Practical Implementation

Implementing recursive nesting requires:

- **A specification for each level** – defining the 12 boundary conditions and the 20 relational nodes.
- **A deployment of System77** – where computational enforcement is required.
- **An attribution ledger** – to track cross‑level contributions.
- **A governance escalation path** – for each level and for inter‑level disputes.

The architecture is modular. A single level can be deployed independently. Additional levels can be added later without redesign.
